Low Level Wizards browser — v53.1 trail tuning

Purpose
Make player-made paths feel accumulated and ecological instead of unlocking after a few passes.

Tuning
- First 4 crossings: no visible trail contribution.
- 5+ crossings: extremely faint scuffing begins through a smooth fade curve rather than a binary reveal.
- ~16 crossings: route begins to read as a deliberate desire line.
- ~32 crossings: route reads as an established footpath.
- 24 crossings through an ordinary bush: vegetation is sufficiently beaten aside to become normal-speed traversal.
- Bushes recover once remembered use falls to 14 or less.
- Player trail strength caps at 48 remembered uses.

Presentation
- Initial route opacity is intentionally tiny (roughly 1–3% depending on exact accumulated use), well below the previous v53 values.
- Continued use slowly darkens and modestly broadens the route.
- Trail rendering remains beneath groundcover so the landscape continues to visually interrupt it.

Decay
- A route gets 2 full unused in-game days with no decay.
- After that grace period, it loses 1 remembered use per unused day.
- Cell wear and actual traversed-edge wear both decay.
- Generated/pre-existing trail truth is preserved as a baseline; only the player's added wear fades.
- A repeatedly used path can therefore persist for many in-game days, while a tentative shortcut quietly disappears.
- Bush wear uses hysteresis: it takes 24 uses to beat a passage open but does not regrow until remembered use falls to 14, avoiding flicker around one threshold.

Acceptance test
Walk the same short route repeatedly. Nothing should pop into existence after one or two trips. Around repeated use, the ground should slowly become legible as a route. Leave that route alone for several in-game days and watch it fade rather than vanish instantly; resume using it and it should strengthen again.
