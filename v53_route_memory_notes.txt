LOW LEVEL WIZARDS BROWSER — v53 ROUTE MEMORY / PLAYER-MADE TRAILS v0

PURPOSE

Prove that repeated travel can become familiarity without adding a navigation stat, waypoint system, or explicit road-building mode.

The generated world already contains desire lines as evidence of prior use. v53 lets the player's own repeated movement enter that same physical grammar. The seed makes the woods; repeated trips begin to make this particular version of the woods yours.

SCOPE

- Record each completed player movement on the destination landscape cell.
- Keep player history separately readable as playerTrailUse/playerTrailAmount.
- Also feed that use into existing trailUse, trailAmount, and disturbance fields.
- Begin showing faint scuffs after 2 crossings.
- Build a clearer desire line around 4 crossings.
- Read as an established footpath around 7 crossings.
- Draw player routes immediately after terrain and before groundcover, so leaves/grass/props remain layered over the path.
- Repeated travel through ordinary bushes physically pushes them lower/wider after 4 crossings.
- A worn ordinary bush route becomes normal-speed traversal.
- Mud remains mud.
- Brambles remain brambles and retain their Vitality consequence.
- Water, crossings, squeezing, rocks, trees, logs, and hard collision remain unchanged.

WORLD TRUTH

Player route memory is not an unlock flag. Cells remember use:

  cell.playerTrailUse
  cell.playerTrailAmount
  cell.trailUse
  cell.trailAmount
  cell.disturbance

Generated historical paths and player-made paths can therefore share presentation/world consequences while remaining distinguishable to later systems.

TUNING DIALS

VISIBLE_USE_THRESHOLD = 2
DESIRE_LINE_THRESHOLD = 4
FOOTPATH_THRESHOLD = 7
BUSH_WORN_THRESHOLD = 4
MAX_PLAYER_TRAIL_USE = 16

These are intentionally fast for prototype testing. The point is to feel a route emerge over a handful of expeditions, not to grind a road into existence.

ACCEPTANCE TEST

1. Start a fresh world.
2. Choose a short route away from camp and walk it out-and-back several times.
3. After the second pass, look for faint ground scuffs.
4. Continue reusing the same cells and verify the line becomes progressively clearer.
5. Include an ordinary bush on the route.
6. Cross that bush four times.
7. Verify the bush visibly squashes/spreads and subsequent crossings use normal movement timing.
8. Step into nearby mud and verify it is still slow.
9. Enter brambles and verify their existing slow/Vitality behavior is unchanged.
10. Regenerate the world and verify the player's route history resets with the new world.

SUCCESS SENTENCE

"I kept taking the same way into the woods. After a few trips I could see where I'd been walking, and the little bush near camp had finally been pushed into a gap I could move through easily."

OUT OF SCOPE

- no navigation skill
- no minimap or waypoint trail
- no road-building tool
- no shovel
- no NPC pathfinding response
- no trail decay yet
- no named routes
- no automatic bramble clearing
- no mud improvement
- no persistent save format yet
